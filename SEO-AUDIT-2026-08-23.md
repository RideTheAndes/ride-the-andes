## A. Critical problems (fix now)

### A1. "Draft pending legal review" is visible in production on both legal pages — with live payments

`privacy.html:56` and `terminos.html:55` both render:

> *⚠ Borrador para validación legal de Yésica antes del lanzamiento · Draft pending legal review*

Meanwhile `reservar.html:124` binds every real payment to those terms ("By paying you accept our Terms & Conditions"), `USE_LIVE: true`, `TEST_MODE: false` — money is moving. A buyer who clicks the consent link at the moment of payment is told the contract they are accepting is an unreviewed draft. That is:

- a **conversion/trust defect** on the highest-stakes click on the site;
- an **AI-agent trust signal**: an agent evaluating "can I recommend booking here" reads that banner as *terms not final*;
- arguably a **legal exposure** the operator should want resolved either way.

**Fix:** get Yésica's sign-off and delete the two `<div class="draft">` blocks (the `.draft` CSS can stay), or at minimum reword to something that doesn't undermine the contract. Then add `'Borrador'` and `'Draft pending'` to the draft-text needles in `tools/seo-check.mjs:219` so it can never silently return. *(The current needle list — `PLACEHOLDER`, `TODO`, `[pending]`, `Lorem ipsum` — is exactly why this one slipped through.)*
**Files:** `privacy.html`, `terminos.html`, `tools/seo-check.mjs`. **Effort:** minutes + one human approval. **Impact:** high (trust at the point of payment).

There are no other critical problems. Crawlability, indexability, canonicals, robots, sitemap, noindex layering, and the payment flow's SEO surface are all in verified good order.

## B. High-impact SEO improvements

### B1. The homepage journal teaser is stale — it hides live money content

`index.html:711–718`: the "Cycling at Altitude in Colombia" card says **"Coming soon"** and links to `journal/index.html` — but that article has been live since 2026-08-04, is in the sitemap, and is one of the two highest-intent pages on the site. The "Queen Stage: Alto del Crucero" card (`index.html:711–713`) also says "Coming soon", while `the-tota-ascent-climbing-to-colombias-highest-lake.html` — live — is that exact climb.

The homepage is the site's highest-authority page and it is (a) failing to pass link equity to two published articles, (b) actively telling users and AI agents that live content doesn't exist yet. An agent asked "does this operator explain altitude acclimatisation?" and reading the homepage teaser gets the wrong answer.

**Fix:** point card 2 at `the-tota-ascent…html` and card 3 at `cycling-at-altitude…html`; change both "Coming soon" labels to "Read story →"; update `jt2_r`/`jt3_r` (and the `jt2_h` framing if desired) in the `ES` dictionary in the same commit, per the i18n rule.
**Files:** `index.html`, `assets/js/site.js`. **Effort:** minutes. **Impact:** high for the effort — internal links from the homepage are the strongest links the site can give.

### B2. All 112 internal cross-page links resolve through 301 redirects

Every internal link between pages targets the *file path*, which `_redirects` then 301s to the canonical URL:

- 81 links point at `index.html` / `../index.html` (+fragments) → 301 → `/`
- 31 links point at `journal/index.html` or (from inside `journal/`) `index.html` → 301 → `/journal/`

The 301 layer exists precisely because Netlify serves `/index.html` as a real file — the redirects are correct and load-bearing. But the internal links shouldn't *rely* on them: every crawl hop and every user click pays a redirect, and link equity consolidates one step later than it needs to. Google copes; it's still friction on 100% of internal navigation.

**Fix:** sitewide replace to root-relative canonical URLs: `href="index.html#tour"` → `/#tour`, `href="../index.html"` → `/`, `href="journal/index.html"` → `/journal/`, journal-internal `href="index.html"` → `/journal/`. Root-relative paths work identically on Netlify and local `python -m http.server`; the only thing they break is `file://` browsing, which this repo's workflow doesn't use. Keep the `_redirects` rules — they still catch external/legacy links.
**Files:** all 13 HTML files. **Effort:** ~1 hour incl. checking each replacement. **Impact:** medium-high (crawl efficiency + removes a whole class of future canonical drift).

### B3. Titles and descriptions that Google truncates (known, still open — cheapest CTR win available)

Unchanged from `FINDINGS-2026-08-09.md` §3–4: five `<title>`s at 68–73 chars (four journal articles + `terminos.html`), four descriptions over 160. The validator flags all of them on every run. This is copywriting, not engineering: trim to ≤60 / ≤155 keeping the location keyword early and the brand last.
**Files:** the five pages. **Effort:** under an hour. **Impact:** direct CTR on exactly the pages meant to win search clicks.

### B4. Image weight on the two pages that matter (known, still open)

`index.html` carries ~5.5 MB of images against the repo's own 3 MB budget; `journal/index.html` ~4.1 MB. Six files do most of the damage (`culture-alto-cogollo-peloton.jpg` 2.1 MB/1848px is the worst). Recompress to `AUDIT.md`'s norm (≤1400px, q75) — same filenames, zero HTML changes, no design risk. This is the 67-vs-97 mobile/desktop Lighthouse gap.
**Files:** 6 JPGs listed by the validator. **Effort:** ~1 hour with any decent encoder. **Impact:** LCP on mobile, where >70% of traffic is.

### B5. Spanish URLs + hreflang — still the single largest lever (known, parked, correctly)

`PLAN-2027.md` Phase 1 already specifies this properly (real `/es/` page generated from the 100%-complete `ES` dictionary, reciprocal hreflang, generator that fails loudly on missing keys). Nothing to add except a scheduling note: **the activation trigger ("October departure closed") arrives Sept 17, 2026 — twenty-five days from this audit.** The work should be staffed now so it starts then. Until `/es/` exists, do not add hreflang tags (pointing hreflang at one URL twice is worse than nothing — already documented, endorsed).

## C. AI / agentic-web improvements

The good news first, because it's substantial: this site is **unusually agent-legible**. All content ships in HTML (no client-side rendering of copy), FAQ answers are in the DOM, every image has a text description via `alt`/`aria-label`, the footer states the legal name, RNT registry number, location and coordinates, and JSON-LD asserts who/where/what/how-much consistently with visible copy — enforced by CI. An agent can answer "what is this, who runs it, where, what does it cost, how do I book" from a single page fetch without executing JavaScript. That is rare.

The gaps:

### C1. The trust counters ship "0" in the HTML

`index.html:209–211`: `<div class="num" data-count="123">0</div>` — the raw markup literally says **"0 Municipalities of Boyacá ridden by our lead guide"** and **"0 Maximum riders per tour"**. JS animates 0→123 on scroll; `<noscript>` *hides* the counters entirely rather than showing the real number. Google renders JS so search is fine — but every non-rendering consumer (many AI crawlers and agent fetches, RSS-style readers, `curl`-grade tools) reads wrong numbers on the site's strongest credibility claims.

**Fix (progressive enhancement, inverted):** ship the real number in the markup (`>123<`), and let JS *start* the animation by zeroing it at animation time. Delete the `[data-count]{visibility:hidden}` noscript rule — no longer needed.
**Files:** `index.html`, `assets/js/site.js`. **Effort:** ~30 min. **Impact:** correct facts for every non-JS reader.

### C2. Articles have no visible date

No journal article shows a publish or updated date anywhere in the visible page — `datePublished` exists only in JSON-LD, and `dateModified` doesn't exist at all. Readers can't judge freshness; agents specifically look for "is this information current" (and the prompt's own question — *"whether information is current"* — is unanswerable from the visible article). It also creates a slow drift risk between schema dates and reality.

**Fix:** add a date `<span>` to the `.ameta` row of each article (e.g. "August 2026"), and add `dateModified` to the `BlogPosting` blocks, updating both when an article materially changes (fold into `AUDIT.md`'s pre-commit ritual).
**Files:** 6 journal articles. **Effort:** ~30 min. **Impact:** freshness signal for humans, Google and agents alike.

### C3. No feed — the journal is invisible to subscription-based discovery

Six articles, monthly cadence promised ("New field notes monthly"), no RSS/Atom. A feed is the cheapest machine-readable endpoint that exists: it powers RSS readers, some AI-crawler discovery, and podcast-style content monitoring. For a no-build site, a hand-maintained `journal/feed.xml` with 6 `<item>`s plus `<link rel="alternate" type="application/rss+xml">` on the journal pages is ~30 minutes, updated once per new article (add to `AUDIT.md`'s new-article checklist).
**Files:** new `journal/feed.xml`, `journal/index.html`, article template. **Effort:** low. **Impact:** modest but permanent discovery channel; strong "machine-readable web" citizenship.

### C4. `robots.txt` and AI crawlers — correct as-is, keep it that way

No `GPTBot`/`ClaudeBot`/`PerplexityBot` blocks exist, meaning AI systems can read and cite the site. For a business whose customers *ask AI where to ride in Colombia*, that is the right posture. Recording it here so a future "block the bots" fashion doesn't undo it silently.

### C5. Optional: `llms.txt`

An emerging (not standardized, uncertain-uptake) convention: a root `llms.txt` summarizing what the site is and pointing at the key pages. Cost is one small text file; benefit is speculative. Worth doing only because the cost is near zero — it is explicitly *not* load-bearing, and nothing should depend on it.

### C6. Entity disambiguation: "Sergio" has no surname anywhere

The founder is "Sergio" in every page and schema block. For knowledge-graph purposes (connecting the person to the Gran Fondo Boyacá Mundial he directs, to press mentions, to RWGPS profiles), a full name in the `founder` property + visible byline would make the Person entity resolvable. **This is an operator privacy/branding decision, not a defect** — flagging, not prescribing.

## D. Technical improvements

### D1. `404.html` violates its own absolute-path rule (2 lines)

`404.html:29–30` — `href="assets/img/favicon.svg"` and the apple-touch-icon are **relative**, on the one page where the repo's own documentation (CLAUDE.md, REPORT.md §1.5b) mandates absolute paths because Netlify serves the 404 body at the requested URL. At `/journal/broken-link` the favicon request 404s. Cosmetic impact, but it's a confirmed rule violation and a two-character fix (`/assets/…`).

### D2. README.md is stale to the point of being wrong

Says 26 images (there are 58), and lists as "pending" four things that shipped long ago (og-image, payment integration, the Ráquira placeholder replacement). CLAUDE.md is the real onboarding doc, but README is what GitHub shows first — and what a future collaborator or AI session reads first. Rewrite in five minutes or delete the Pendiente section.

### D3. `sitemap.xml` still carries `<changefreq>`/`<priority>`

Google has ignored both since 2023; the repo's own validator warns about them on every run. Removing them silences a permanent warning and makes `<lastmod>` — the signal that *does* matter and is CI-validated — the only thing the operator maintains.

### D4. Security headers: add HSTS

`_headers` already sets nosniff/referrer/permissions/frame headers (good). Missing: `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains`. The site is HTTPS-only behind Cloudflare; HSTS is free hardening. (A full CSP is *not* recommended casually here: the PayPal SDK, Plausible and Google Fonts make a correct policy nontrivial, and a wrong one breaks the payment page. If attempted, do it in report-only mode first, on a deploy preview.)

### D5. `terminos.html` head inconsistencies (known, from PLAN-2027 §4)

Only page with `<meta charset>` after the Plausible scripts and with a render-blocking font `<link>` (no preload/print-swap pattern). Harmless in practice (charset is within the first 1024 bytes), but it's the one head that doesn't match the house standard — normalize it next time the file is open anyway.

### D6. Performance profile (confirmed healthy, for the record)

JS is ~14 KB unminified with no dependencies; CSS ~24 KB single file; fonts use preload+swap; hero LCP has `srcset`, `fetchpriority="high"`, explicit dimensions (no CLS); RWGPS iframe is lazy; below-fold backgrounds are IntersectionObserver-deferred; mobile skips the hero rotation entirely (documented DPR analysis in `site.js`). The only real performance debt is B4 (image weight). TTFB/INP are non-issues for a static site with this little JS. The `body::after` fixed-position grain overlay with `mix-blend-mode` is a mild compositing cost on low-end devices — acceptable, not worth removing for taste.

### D7. Journal template duplication (documented, accept until there's a generator)

Seven journal files each carry ~160 duplicated lines of inline CSS (documented tradeoff in CLAUDE.md). Fine at 7 files; at 15+ articles it becomes the argument for the same small generator that Phase 1 (Spanish) introduces anyway. No action now — just don't hand-fork an eighth copy without considering it.
